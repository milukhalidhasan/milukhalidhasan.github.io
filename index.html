01 — About

From sky and canopy down to the field

Research interests

UAV and controlled-environment plant phenotyping · crop drought and heat stress · time-series canopy phenology (double-logistic) · thermal and multispectral sensing · machine learning with cross-season validation · physiological ground-truthing of image traits

I am an agricultural scientist working on UAV-based plant phenotyping of crop drought stress.

In my MS research I ran a two-season wheat drought trial, measured leaf physiology by hand, and turned multispectral and thermal UAV time series into stress and yield phenotypes using double-logistic curves and machine learning, with validation across seasons. I currently work as a Project Scientific Officer at the Bangladesh Rice Research Institute.

UAV-Based Impact Assessment of Seaweed Extract (Gracilaria spp.) and Nitrogen Fertilizer on Wheat Growth and Yield under Drought Stress Using Machine Learning and Double Logistic Model

MS thesis, Gazipur Agricultural University. Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Hasan M. Abdullah (Gazipur Agricultural University; currently a postdoctoral researcher at Texas Tech University). Co-supervisor: Dr. Md. Farhadur Rahman.
Two-season split-plot field trial (2024–25, 2025–26): irrigated vs managed drought × 4 nitrogen rates × 3 seaweed-extract doses, 4 replications, 96 plots per season.

  • 2 seasons
  • 96 plots per season
  • 11 and 10 UAV flight dates
  • 38 vegetation indices (six used for double-logistic phenology)

  • UAV data beat destructive sampling

    UAV canopy data predicted grain yield better than destructive sampling (R² 0.82 vs 0.72); one flight at 70–80 days after sowing gave r ≈ 0.85 in both seasons.

  • Calibrate by season

    Yield models needed season-specific calibration to work in a new season.

  • Drought detected in a new season

    Double-logistic parameters detected drought plots in a new season (balanced accuracy 0.84, vs 0.61 for raw trajectories); drought advanced mid-senescence by 4–12 days.

  • Thermal shows the stress

    UAV thermal: drought plots were 1.6–3.3 °C warmer, matching 35% lower stomatal conductance; a thermal stress index correlated with grain yield (r = −0.69).

Key results: unpublished, manuscript in preparation. Figures are from the first season.
